Biography

Marco Martalò is Associate Professor of Telecommunications at the Department of Electrical and Electronic Engineering (DIEE) of the University of Cagliari, Italy, where he is a member of the Networks for Humans (Net4U) laboratory. He received the Laurea (2003) and Laurea Specialistica summa cum laude (2005) degrees in Telecommunications Engineering, and the Ph.D. in Information Technologies (2009), all from the University of Parma, Italy, under the supervision of Prof. Gianluigi Ferrari. Between October 2007 and March 2008 he was a Visiting Scholar at the School of Computer and Communication Sciences of the École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne (EPFL), Switzerland.

He held post-doctoral and assistant professor positions at the University of Parma and E-Campus University between 2009 and 2020, before joining the University of Cagliari as Associate Professor in December 2020. His research interests span trustworthy and secure networking, advanced applications for communication networks — with a focus on the Internet of Things — and audio signal processing, building on an earlier career in distributed detection and transmission over non-ideal channels.

Prof. Martalò is author of more than 130 scientific publications, with an h-index of 18 (Google Scholar) and 16 (Scopus), and is co-inventor of two Italian/international patents. He co-authored the book “Sensor Networks with IEEE 802.15.4 Systems: Distributed Processing, MAC, and Connectivity” (Springer, 2011) and co-edited the CNIT Technical Report-11 “Towards a Trustworthy Information Exchange in the Digital Era” (2023). He is an IEEE Senior Member and holds the Italian National Scientific Qualification for Full Professorship in Telecommunications.

He currently serves as Associate Editor for IEEE Networking Letters, IEEE Communications Letters, and IEEE Access, and as General Chair of the IEEE International Mediterranean Conference on Communications and Networking (MeditCom 2026, Cagliari). He is Secretary of the IEEE Communications Society Italy Chapter (2026–2027) and a frequent invited speaker, tutorial lecturer, and TPC member for IEEE conferences including ICC, Globecom, and PIMRC.
